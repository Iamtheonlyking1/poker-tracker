Key principle: Smaller sizes → higher frequency, more bluffs. Larger sizes → more polarised, fewer combos. Match size to your range's polarity, not just your hand strength.

Ace in your hand
Removes 3 combos of AA (from 6 → 3) — villain less likely to hold the nuts in 3-bet pots
Blocks AKs, AKo, AQs — villain has fewer premium Ax holdings to 4-bet-call with
Great 4-bet bluff blocker. Also blocks villain's strong calling hands → good bluff candidate
King in your hand
Blocks KK (6 → 3 combos), KQs, KJs, KTs — reduces villain's strong broadways
Good 3-bet bluff card from BTN vs UTG open — unblocks villain's folding range (no Kx-heavy folds)
Flush card in hand (e.g. 5♥ on K♥ T♥ 3♦)
Each suited card you hold removes that card from villain's flush draw combos
Holding A♥ on a 3-heart board → villain cannot have the nut flush, making bluff-catching easier
Bluffing with a flush blocker on the river is a core GTO concept (you block their value, they fold bluff-catchers)
Straight card in hand (e.g. 9 on J-T-8)
Holding 9 on J-T-8 blocks the nut straight (QJ9x / 97x combos reduced ~50%)
Good bluffing card when you block villain's nuts AND unblock their weak hands (the hands they'd fold)
The "Unblocking" principle
Good bluffs should UNBLOCK villain's folding range — don't hold the cards villain needs to fold a hand
Example: bluff with 6♥5♥ on K♠7♣2♦ — 65s doesn't block top pair (Kx, 7x) which villain would fold to a bet
Avoid bluffing K♥6♥ on K-7-2 — you hold a K which reduces villain's Kx folds (they can't have as many Kx)
MDF — Minimum Defence Frequency
Formula: MDF = Pot ÷ (Pot + Bet). Half-pot bet → MDF = 67%. Pot-size bet → MDF = 50%.
Fold more than (1 − MDF) of your range and villain profits with any two cards as a bluff
Use MDF to build calling ranges vs large bets, not to call every hand — mix in raises and folds appropriately
Alpha — Break-Even Bluff Frequency
Formula: α = Bet ÷ (Pot + Bet). Half-pot bluff needs 33% fold equity. Pot-size needs 50%.
If you estimate villain folds more than α, the bluff is immediately profitable regardless of your hand
Range Advantage & Nut Advantage
Range advantage: whose range connects better with the board overall (average equity)
Nut advantage: who has more combos of the strongest hands. More important for sizing — nuttier range can use larger sizes
IP has range + nut advantage on dry high boards (A-7-2). BB has range advantage on low connected boards (7-6-5)
Polarization vs Condensed Range
Polar: only nuts + bluffs → prefer large sizes (pot, overbet)
Condensed: medium-strength hands, no nutted combos → prefer small sizes to protect vs raises
3-bet pot caller has a condensed range (no AA/KK). 3-bettor is polar. This drives who bets large postflop.
GTO vs Exploitative Play
GTO: unexploitable equilibrium — the safe baseline vs unknowns
vs Fish (VPIP 50+, low PFR): remove bluffs, value-bet every street thinner, size up vs calling stations
vs Nit (<15% VPIP): 3-bet wider, steal every unopened pot, fold to 3-bets unless premiums
Use GTO as a baseline, deviate as soon as you have two or more reliable reads on a player
